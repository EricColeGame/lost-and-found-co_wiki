## Start With Smarter Searching, Not Faster Clicking

If you are down to one missing item and the room insists it is still there, clicking harder will not fix it. **Lost and Found Co.** is a hidden object adventure built on crowded shelves, overlapping clutter, and props that hide the real target underneath, so most misses come from scanning habits and awkward hitboxes rather than bad eyesight. The fix is a repeatable process: slow down, sweep the scene in sections, click small items near their center, and save your Juju hints for the last stubborn object instead of the first annoying moment.

That structure matters because the game layers several goal types on top of each other. Story Cases push the main adventure forward, Requests behave like side jobs, optional objectives add extra targets inside a level, and Juju are hidden helpers that make later searches easier. Knowing which type you are actually hunting changes how you should look.

## The Layer-by-Layer Sweep That Finds Almost Anything

The most reliable habit in the game is to treat every scene as stacked layers instead of one flat picture. Clear them in a fixed order so you never half-search five zones and remember none of them properly.

- **Top layer:** skyline details, hanging props, signage, and anything suspended above eye level.
- **Middle layer:** shelves, counters, trays, drawers, and box lips where small items get tucked into seams.
- **Bottom layer:** floor clutter, corners, and the messy edges of the scene.
- **Interior zones:** if the map has multiple floors or separate rooms, finish one zone completely before moving to the next.

Two mechanical habits support that sweep. First, zoom in whenever a target looks tiny, and pan across packed areas instead of clicking blindly. Second, change floors or rooms the moment furniture starts stacking on itself, because the clutter is usually hiding a transition rather than a target. The game also likes placing items inside the edges of signs, shelf lips, window frames, trays, drawers, and boxes — props that need one extra click before the actual object appears.

Finally, aim for the center of small items. A lot of reported misses come from clicking the outer edge of a tiny object with a tight hitbox, not from misreading the clue.

## Reading Objective Types: Obscured, Quest, and Juju

Once you know what kind of objective you are chasing, the room stops feeling random. The three types behave very differently, and mixing them up is a common reason players stall out.

| Objective type | What it means | How to handle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Obscured** | The item is hidden inside or behind something clickable | Open the prop first — vague clues usually mean "look under this" |
| **Quest** | Finding one target unlocks more targets in a chain | Do not assume the room is finished when the first item disappears |
| **Juju** | An optional objective disguised as an ordinary object | Catching one grants extra hints for later item hunts |

If a clue sounds strangely worded, trust the shape over the label. Localization can make an object name read oddly, but the game still plays visually fair — a folded bill can register as a wallet, and a broad-sounding clue usually clicks into place once you search the right prop cluster.

### When to spend Juju hints

Juju are easy to burn out of frustration, and that is the worst possible time to use them. Spend them after the broad sweep, when you have narrowed the problem to one item or one zone, so the hint points you toward the correct corner instead of confirming what you already knew. Treat them as a finisher, not an opener.

## The Clean 100% Route for Achievements and Requests

Full completion here is refreshingly undramatic. Casual Game Guides reported in April 2026 that the then-current Steam build had 27 achievements, each tied to 100% completion of a specific stage, with no missable route splits or speedrun tricks. Rather than forcing perfection on a first visit, the calmer route looks like this:

1. Play the story once so you learn how each room likes to hide things.
2. Replay stages with unfinished side tasks while the layout is still fresh in your head.
3. Catch the Juju and clear optional objectives on those replays.
4. Save the most clutter-heavy maps for relaxed cleanup sessions.

Expect the busiest cleanup runs in Hop Street, Jolly Jug Isle, Nekoten Cafe, Breezy Hill Heights, Leo Family's Home, and Kenta's House. Also note that not every request-style stage carries its own achievement — that same guide lists Clubhouse of the Dead, The End of Summer Memories, The Stumbling Steps, Fangtastic Feast, Master of Findjutsu, Finding Familiars, and Duckinator 3029 as stages without separate rewards. If an achievement name looks unfamiliar, trust the stage it is attached to; a few labels use shorter or older naming than the stage pages do.

## Community Guides and Known Quirks Worth Checking

When a single item refuses to cooperate, the Steam Community guide hub for [Lost and Found Co. on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2101390/) is the fastest place to cross-reference. Player-made resources there include a full main and optional objectives list, a Requests guide for mission objects, screenshot-based item locations, and dedicated Juju location guides — including an updated version covering the Autumn content.

Community reports are also worth reading for bugs, though treat them as reports rather than confirmed patch state. One widely rated Steam guide (posted June 24, 2026) warns that a shuriken objective on the tower may fail to count if you trigger a shuriken before accepting the mission, and recommends talking to the NPC immediately on entering the map. That same guide notes the issue had been reported and was pending a fix at the time of writing — so check whether your build still behaves that way before assuming a level is broken.

## FAQ

**What is the fastest way to find the last missing object in Lost and Found Co.?**

Stop scanning the whole scene and re-sweep one zone at a time, top to bottom. Open any clickable props near where the clue seems to point, since Obscured objectives hide the real item underneath. Only after that should you spend a Juju hint.

**Are Juju hints limited, and should I save them?**

Treat them as limited and save them. They are far more useful when you are down to one or two stubborn items in a known zone than when a room is still full of unfound clutter.

**Do these Lost and Found Co. hints apply to 100% completion runs?**

Yes. The same loop — story first, then replays for optional objectives and Juju — is the recommended path to the game's stage-based achievements, and it keeps the clutter-heavy maps from turning into a grind.

**Why did an objective not count even though I found the item?**

In some cases it is a known bug rather than player error. Community guides have flagged at least one mission where triggering an object before accepting the quest can prevent it from counting, so pick up objectives from the NPC first when you enter a map.
