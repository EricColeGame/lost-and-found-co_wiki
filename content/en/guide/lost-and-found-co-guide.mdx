## What a Good Lost and Found Co. Guide Fixes

Lost and Found Co. is a cozy hidden object adventure built around a simple but sneaky loop: you hunt for lost items across crowded, layered scenes, solve small puzzles, and help a tiny dragon named Ducky work her way back to full power. The frustration never comes from the game being unfair — it comes from a single item vanishing into visual clutter while everything else looks found.

That is the problem this guide solves. Below you will find the search habits that cut down wasted clicks, a plain explanation of how the game's objective types behave, a sensible policy for spending Juju hints, and the route experienced players recommend for 100% completion and the achievement list.

One note before you start: this is a live game. The developer has shipped content updates through 2026, so treat specific counts and stage names as accurate to the version noted, not as permanent facts.

## Core Loop: Story Cases, Requests, Optional Objectives, and Juju

The structure settles in quickly once you know the four moving parts. Casual Game Guides' [Lost and Found Co. walkthrough](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/lost-and-found-co) breaks them down clearly:

- **Story Cases** push the main adventure forward. These are your progression path.
- **Requests** behave like side jobs — separate stages with their own item lists.
- **Optional objectives** are bonus goals tucked inside a level you are already playing.
- **Juju** are hidden helpers disguised as ordinary objects. Catching one gives you extra hints for later hunts.

If you only want to keep moving, clear the main objective and leave. If you like full clears, come back for optional goals once you already know the room's layout. The office decorating between cases gives you a visible sense of progress while you work through both.

### Objective types and how they behave

| Objective type | How it works | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Obscured | The item sits inside or behind something clickable | Open the prop first, then look again |
| Quest | Finding one chain item unlocks more targets | Do not assume the room is done after one find |
| Juju | Optional helper disguised as a normal object | Catch it for extra hints later |

The practical takeaway: when a clue feels too vague to act on, that is usually a signal that the answer is behind an interactive prop rather than plainly visible on a shelf.

## How to Search Crowded Rooms Without Wasting Clicks

The instinct when you are stuck is to click faster. It rarely helps. The better habit is to slow down and search in layers, because Lost and Found Co. builds its scenes in stacks — skyline and hanging props at the top, shelves and counters in the middle, floor clutter at the bottom. Clear one zone completely before moving to the next. Half-searching five areas is how you end up remembering none of them.

Use the camera tools more than you think you need to. Zoom in on anything tiny, pan across dense areas instead of clicking blind, and switch floors as soon as furniture starts overlapping itself. Many misses are not perception failures at all — they come from awkward hitboxes and objects that are only partly visible. Clicking near the center of a small item lands far more reliably than clicking its edge.

Finally, remember that the game hides things inside props: the edges of signs, shelf lips, window frames, trays, drawers, and boxes. Some of those need one extra click before the real target appears.

## When to Spend Juju (and When Not To)

Juju are tempting the moment a room annoys you, but spending them early wastes their value. They work best as a finisher, not a starter. Do your full sweep first, narrow the problem to one stubborn item or one stubborn zone, and then let the hint point you toward the right corner of the room.

If you keep missing the same type of object, trust shape over wording. Localization can make an item name sound stranger than the thing actually is, and the game still plays fair visually. A folded bill can register as a wallet; a tiny prop can be tucked into a shelf seam. When a clue sounds broad, search the prop cluster it most likely belongs to rather than the whole scene again.

## The 100% Completion Route and the Achievement List

According to Casual Game Guides' [100% completion and achievement breakdown](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/lost-and-found-co/completion-achievements), the Steam build at the time of that April 2026 write-up had **27 achievements**, and every one was tied to earning 100% completion on a specific stage — no speedrun tricks, no missable route splits, no hidden combat challenge. Completing a stage fully means finishing the main objective, clearing optional tasks, and catching the Juju so nothing is left greyed out.

The recommended order is deliberately undramatic:

1. Play the story through once so you learn how each room likes to hide things.
2. Replay stages with unfinished side tasks while the layout is still fresh in your memory.
3. Save Juju for the last stubborn object or zone.
4. Expect the heaviest cleanup runs in Hop Street, Jolly Jug Isle, Nekoten Cafe, Breezy Hill Heights, Leo Family's Home, and Kenta's House.

Named achievement examples from that list include **Unlucky Ducky** and **The Sus Fox** for story stages, plus **Waiting for a Bite** and **Tea Time Troubles** for request stages. Notably, several request-style stages — including Clubhouse of the Dead, The End of Summer Memories, The Stumbling Steps, Fangtastic Feast, Master of Findjutsu, Finding Familiars, and Duckinator 3029 — did not have their own Steam achievements in that snapshot. If an achievement label looks unfamiliar, trust the stage it is tied to; a few use shorter or older naming than the stage pages do.

For chapter-by-chapter coverage, the [Into Indie Games walkthrough hub](https://intoindiegames.com/walkthrough-hub/lost-and-found-co-walkthrough-hub/) splits the game into Chapters 1–7, Chapters 8–9 (Muddy Situation, Smoothie Showdown, Missing Meow, My Summer Memories, The Accursed Antique Shop), and a separate KOZ side quest section covering Apartment Apocalypse, Waiting for a Bite, and Playtime at Kenta's.

## Known Issues, Patch Notes, and Platform News

The developer posts patch notes directly on the [official Steam community hub for Lost and Found Co.](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2101390). Patch 1.2.1, posted October 14, added items to the Kit Shop, updated localization text, fixed a loading-screen crash, fixed a bug where the to-do UI could not be opened after a quest unlock, corrected saved object transforms, and applied stage fixes to the Lobby, Love After Life, and Fangtastic Feast.

There is also a community-reported bug worth knowing about. A Steam guide author publishing under the name 小蓝洗衣机 warned that in the newer stage content, triggering a shuriken on the tower before accepting the related mission may cause it not to count, potentially blocking level completion. That report came from a player guide, not from the developer, and was described as reported and pending a fix — so accept the mission from the NPC first if you are working through that content.

On the platform side, the team announced on October 28, 2026 that Lost and Found Co. is coming to Nintendo Switch 2 and Nintendo Switch in early 2027, with co-op for up to four players plus Joy-Con 2 mouse controls, motion controls, and touchscreen support.

## FAQ

**Is there a Lost and Found Co. guide that covers optional objectives, not just the main story?**
Yes. The most useful community resources are full objective lists and screenshot compilations on Steam, plus chapter walkthroughs that separate main cases from requests and optional goals. Start with the story, then use those lists for cleanup runs.

**How many achievements does Lost and Found Co. have?**
The Steam build covered by Casual Game Guides in April 2026 had 27 achievements, all tied to 100% stage completion. Because the game receives content updates, check the current global achievements page before planning a completion run.

**What are Juju, and should I save them?**
Juju are hidden helpers disguised as ordinary objects that grant extra hints once caught. Save them for the last one or two missing items in a room rather than spending them the moment you get stuck.

**Does this Lost and Found Co. guide apply to the Switch version?**
The search and objective advice carries over, but the Switch release is announced for early 2027 and has not shipped yet. Achievement counts and patch-specific fixes described here apply to the Steam version.
