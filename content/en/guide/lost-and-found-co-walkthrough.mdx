You can sweep an entire room in Lost and Found Co. and still spend ten minutes hunting the one item the counter insists is there. The fix is rarely faster clicking — it's knowing how the game labels objectives, which props hide things, and when to spend a Juju instead of your patience. This walkthrough covers the game's structure, a search method that works, a sensible stage order, and the documented route to 100% completion.

## How Lost and Found Co Is Structured

Lost and Found Co. is a hidden object adventure built around cozy, hand-drawn locations and a cast of characters you help across many small cases. The official [Lost and Found Co. Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2101390/) describes the premise plainly: find lost items, solve puzzles, and help a tiny dragon regain her power.

Between cases you return to an office that you decorate, which is where a lot of the sense of progress lives. Patch 1.2.1 (October 14, 2026) even added a gamepad workaround so players could name Ducky's Rooms when saving, and cleaned up the objects surrounding the office.

Once you settle in, the content splits into four buckets:

- **Story Cases** — the main adventure, played in order.
- **Requests** — side jobs that reuse locations with their own target lists.
- **Optional objectives** — extra goals tucked inside a level you're already in.
- **Juju** — hidden collectibles that double as a hint resource.

If you just want to keep moving, chase the main objective and leave the optional cleanup for a second visit, when you already know how the room hides things.

## How to Search Crowded Rooms Without Wasting Time

Most "impossible" items aren't invisible. They're buried in clutter, tucked behind a clickable prop, or sitting on a hitbox that punishes sloppy clicks. Three habits fix the majority of stalls.

### Scan in layers, not in circles

Work through every scene in a fixed order: skyline and hanging props first, then shelves and counters, then floor clutter. On maps with multiple floors or interior rooms, clear one zone completely before moving to the next. The usual failure isn't bad eyesight — it's half-searching five areas and remembering none of them.

### Use zoom and pan more than you think

Zoom in when a target looks tiny, pan across packed areas instead of clicking blind, and switch floors as soon as furniture starts stacking on itself. When you do click a small item, aim for its center; misses often come from awkward hitboxes rather than from the object being absent.

### Open the props first

Lost and Found Co. hides targets inside the edges of signs, shelf lips, window frames, trays, drawers, and boxes — anything that needs one extra click before the real object appears. If a clue feels oddly vague, the answer is frequently "open the thing nearest to where you're looking."

## Objective Types Explained: Obscured, Quest, and Juju

Knowing which kind of objective you're stuck on changes what you should do next.

| Objective type | What it means | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|
| Obscured | The item is hidden inside or behind something clickable | Open or move the prop before searching the open room again |
| Quest | Finding one target unlocks further targets in a chain | Don't assume the room is finished when the first item disappears |
| Juju | An optional collectible disguised as an ordinary object | Grab them when convenient; spend the resulting hints late |

That last row matters. Hints are most valuable when you've already narrowed the problem to one stubborn item or one stubborn corner. Burning them on a room you haven't properly swept just wastes them.

One more tip worth internalizing: trust shape over wording. Localization can make an object name sound stranger than the object looks, but the art plays fair. A folded bill can satisfy a wallet clue, and a broad-sounding request usually makes sense once you find the right prop cluster.

## Stage Order, Requests, and 100% Completion

There's no single official walkthrough document, but the community has mapped the campaign well. The [Into Indie Games Lost and Found Co. walkthrough hub](https://intoindiegames.com/walkthrough-hub/lost-and-found-co-walkthrough-hub/) splits coverage into three parts:

- **Chapters 1–7**, plus the Runaway Foxes side quest, with dedicated help for Toy Time Panic and What an Unlucky Day.
- **Chapters 8–9**, covering Muddy Situation and Smoothie Showdown, along with Missing Meow, My Summer Memories, and The Accursed Antique Shop.
- **KOZ side quests**, including Apartment Apocalypse and Waiting for a Bite, plus Playtime at Kenta's.

A workable order for most players: play the story straight through once, replay stages with unfinished side tasks while the layout is still fresh, and save the clutter-heavy maps for calm sessions rather than forcing perfection on a first visit.

For achievements, the picture is simple. According to [Casual Game Guides' achievement breakdown](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/lost-and-found-co/completion-achievements), published in April 2026, the Steam build carried 27 achievements and every one was tied to 100% completion of a specific stage — no speedrun tricks, no missable route splits. Their listed examples include **Unlucky Ducky**, **The Sus Fox**, **Waiting for a Bite**, and **Tea Time Troubles**.

That same breakdown notes several request-style stages had no separate achievement at the time, among them Clubhouse of the Dead, The End of Summer Memories, The Stumbling Steps, Fangtastic Feast, Master of Findjutsu, Finding Familiars, and Duckinator 3029. It also flags the busiest cleanup runs as Hop Street, Jolly Jug Isle, Nekoten Cafe, Breezy Hill Heights, Leo Family's Home, and Kenta's House.

Because the game has received level additions since that list was published — including two new levels in a June 24, 2026 update and an Autumn update — check the live achievement list before planning a final completion run rather than trusting an older count.

In a June 24, 2026 update guide, Steam user 小蓝洗衣机 warns that triggering a shuriken on the tower before accepting the related mission may stop it from counting, potentially blocking level completion. On the official side, patch 1.2.1 fixed several things that could interrupt a run: the To-do UI failing to open after a quest unlock, object scale and rotation not applying correctly on load, a stage soft lock in Love After Life, and a slime objective not displaying correctly when re-entering Fangtastic Feast. Separately, the developers announced on October 28, 2026 that the game is coming to Nintendo Switch 2 and Nintendo Switch in early 2027, with co-op for up to four players and mouse, motion, and touchscreen control options.

## FAQ

**Is there a complete Lost and Found Co walkthrough in one place?**
Not from the developers. Your best combination is a chapter-based hub for story progression plus a Steam guide for objective and Juju locations. Cross-reference both when a stage refuses to hit 100%.

**Do I need 100% on every stage for achievements?**
Based on the April 2026 achievement breakdown, yes — each of the 27 achievements corresponded to full completion of a specific stage. Confirm the current list in-game, since levels have been added since then.

**What should I do when I'm stuck on one last item?**
Re-sweep the room in layers, open every container and prop edge, and zoom in on shelf seams and sign borders. Only then spend a Juju hint — by that point it can actually point you somewhere useful.

**What are Juju, and should I save them?**
They're optional collectibles disguised as ordinary objects that feed into the hint system. Pick them up as you go, but hold the hints until you're down to one stubborn target rather than using them the moment a room annoys you.
