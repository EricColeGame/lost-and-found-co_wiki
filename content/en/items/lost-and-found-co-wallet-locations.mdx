## Lost and Found Co Wallet Locations: The Short Answer

If you are down to your final wallet in Lost and Found Co., you are almost certainly in the **Missing Meow** chapter, and the item is probably not where you think it is. Community walkthroughs document three wallet-related search spots: inside the yellow-and-white tube, tucked into the pillows below the cat lady painting, and around the café seating near the lost-and-found bin.

That is the honest scope of what is currently verified. No public guide publishes a numbered, screenshot-by-screenshot wallet database yet, so treat the spots below as the confirmed problem areas rather than a full collectible list. The good news is that these three locations are exactly the ones that stall most players.

The first two spots come from the Casual Game Guides breakdown of hard-to-find items in Lost and Found Co., which specifically flags the tube wallet as the one that "burns the most time." The café seating and lost-and-found bin suggestion comes from Tposegaming's Missing Meow guide, which lists finding the wallet among the chapter's optional side objectives.

## How to Get the Wallet Out of the Tube

This is the step that breaks most runs. The tube is not a single click — it is a small sequence:

1. Click the yellow-and-white tube repeatedly until it has rolled fully clear of whatever is blocking it.
2. Open the cap on the tube.
3. Check the inside of the tube itself for the wallet.

If you clicked the tube once, saw nothing, and moved on, you have not actually checked it. Casual Game Guides describes this as the single biggest time sink in the chapter, and it fits the game's broader design habit of hiding targets inside props that need one extra interaction before the real object appears.

## The Pillow Wallet That Disappears Into the Pattern

Still short one wallet after clearing the tube? Head to the pillows below the cat lady painting. A second wallet can sit in the bedding and visually merge with the fabric pattern, which is why players walk past it on multiple laps.

Two habits help here. First, zoom in before you click — the game's smaller props are easy to skim at default zoom. Second, click the center of suspicious shapes rather than the edges, since awkward hitboxes account for a lot of near-misses in crowded scenes. If a pillow looks slightly "off" in its pattern, that is your cue.

## Sweep the Café Seating and the Lost-and-Found Bin

Tposegaming's Missing Meow walkthrough recommends clearing the optional side objectives first, because they thin out the map and make the main hunt easier. Its checklist places the wallet near the café seating and the lost-and-found bin, alongside objectives like the secret mouse party, the elusive cat lady, the missing ducklings, the toy mice, and the birthday surprise.

Working the side objectives before the cats is a sensible order regardless. You learn the room layout, you open up hidden areas, and you stop confusing "I haven't searched here" with "there's nothing here."

## Why Wallets Are So Easy to Miss in This Game

A few structural quirks in Lost and Found Co. explain the frustration:

- **Objects hide inside objects.** Tubes roll, caps open, drawers slide. The visible prop is often a container, not the target.
- **The clue wording can mislead.** Casual Game Guides notes that a folded bill can count as a wallet, so trust the shape more than the label.
- **Clutter overlaps.** Signs, shelf lips, window frames, trays, and boxes all create edges where small items vanish.
- **Partial visibility is normal.** Many misses are objects that are only partly on screen, not objects you failed to recognize.

When you are missing exactly one item, the most reliable fix is to stop doing full-room laps. Re-read the objective, pick the single prop cluster that best matches the clue, and interact with every layered object in that cluster before moving on. In this game, the extra click is usually the answer.

## When a Wallet Objective Won't Resolve

If an objective appears stuck, check your build before you assume you missed something. Casual Game Guides documented a softlock tied to the "Talk to Mallet" objective that was addressed in patch 1.0.5 — that fix is about Mallet, not wallets, so do not expect it to solve a missing wallet on its own.

For anything the written guides do not cover, the Steam Community guide hub for Lost and Found Co. is the fastest place to check player-made screenshot and video walkthroughs. ## FAQ

**How many wallets are in Lost and Found Co.?**
Community guides confirm wallet objectives in the Missing Meow chapter and describe "the last wallet" as a distinct problem item, but no verified public source gives a complete numbered total. The three confirmed search spots are the yellow-and-white tube, the pillows below the cat lady painting, and the café seating near the lost-and-found bin.

**Does a folded bill count as a wallet?**
Yes, according to Casual Game Guides, a folded bill can register as a wallet. That is why Lost and Found Co wallet locations sometimes look like ordinary paper clutter when you finally spot them.

**Why won't the wallet come out of the yellow-and-white tube?**
You likely have not finished the interaction chain. Keep clicking the tube until it rolls fully clear, then open the cap and check inside the tube body.

**Where should I look if I'm still missing one wallet after all three spots?**
Re-check the pillows under the cat lady painting with zoom on, and sweep the café seating and lost-and-found bin again while clicking layered props in the center. If nothing turns up, verify your game version and compare against a current screenshot guide on the Steam Community hub.
