## What the Location Guides Actually Track

If your real goal is finding hidden objects, the objective types matter more than any single item list. Casual Game Guides' walkthrough breaks them down clearly, and this is the framework to search with.

| Objective type | What it means | How to approach it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Obscured | Hidden inside or behind something clickable | Click or open the prop before assuming the item is missing |
| Quest | Finding one target unlocks more targets | Don't leave the level when the first chain item vanishes |
| Juju | Optional helpers disguised as ordinary objects | Collect them to earn extra hints for later hunts |

Juju are the closest thing the game has to a currency-like collectible: they function as stored hints rather than purchasable items, so spending them carelessly early is the main mistake the walkthrough warns against.

## How to Find a Stubborn Item Without a List

When you're down to one missing object, the walkthrough's advice is to slow down rather than click faster:

1. **Scan in layers.** Check the skyline and hanging props first, then shelves and counters, then floor clutter.
2. **Clear one zone at a time.** If the scene has multiple floors or interior rooms, finish one before moving on.
3. **Zoom and pan deliberately.** Tiny objects sit inside sign edges, shelf lips, window frames, trays, drawers, and boxes.
4. **Open the prop.** If a clue sounds vague, the target is often inside or behind a clickable object.
5. **Hold your hints.** Spend Juju only once you've narrowed the problem to one item or one corner of the room.

One more practical note: translation differences can make an object name sound unfamiliar. Trust the shape the clue implies — a folded bill can satisfy a wallet objective — and search the matching prop cluster carefully.
