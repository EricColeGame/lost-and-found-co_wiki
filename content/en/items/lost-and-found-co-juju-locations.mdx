## What Juju Are and Why Their Locations Matter

Juju are optional collectibles tucked into the clutter of almost every stage in *Lost and Found Co.*, and each one you grab becomes a stronger hint you can spend later. That makes their locations worth knowing: a Juju you walk past is a hint you never get to use on the one item that refuses to show up. The catch is that the game never marks them on a map. You find them by reading the scenery, not by following a marker, and the community's location guides are built almost entirely from screenshots and video because the spots are so visual.

If you are searching for a written list of coordinates, that does not really exist for this game — and any article claiming otherwise is guessing. What does exist is a small set of player-made guides that walk each level in order, plus a handful of spotting tricks that make the hunt much faster.

## How to Spot a Juju Before You Check a Map

The most useful advice comes from the Steam community rather than from the game itself. A long-running spoiler thread on the [Lost and Found Co. Steam discussions board](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2101390/discussions/0/563626883365774428/) lays out the visual tells that separate a Juju from ordinary scenery:

- **They are grey, not colorful.** If you think you have clicked every grey prop in a room, click them again — slowly.
- **Their outlines wobble.** Jujus literally cannot sit still; their object borders shift slightly, which is the single most reliable way to pick one out of a crowded shelf.
- **They come in all sizes.** Some are tiny enough to hide in a seam; others are large enough that players overlook them because they assume something that big must be part of the background.
- **Some need a second click.** A few Jujus only appear after you interact with the prop in front of them, so a room is never fully searched until you have opened the obvious containers.
- **A few sit in secret areas.** If a stage has a hidden room or a spot you have not revealed yet, that is a prime suspect.

The same thread's author recommends working through hints gradually rather than jumping straight to the answer screenshots, since the hunt is the point of the game.

## Juju Locations by Level: What the Community Guides Cover

Steam's [Lost and Found Co. guides hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2101390/guides/) currently hosts two dedicated Juju references, and both organize their screenshots by level rather than by a single mega-map:

- **"All Juju Locations Guide (with Autumn Update)" by Lotus** — described by its author as a complete guide to where Jujus are found, ordered chronologically so you can follow along as you unlock stages.
- **"All Juju Locations" by Bliccer** — the older of the two, built from the demo but noted by its author as still covering Jujus that carried over into the full release.

The demo-era thread lists hint screenshots for the levels it covered at the time: **First Request** and **The Missing Captain** on the main story path, plus the side requests **Runaway Foxes**, **Tea Time Troubles**, and **Missing Meow**. Treat those specific screenshots as historical — the author posted them in June 2025 and later had to reupload the images after other players reported they had stopped loading, so if a link looks broken, check the newer guides first.

For stages beyond that early set, the broader [Lost and Found Co. walkthrough index on Casual Game Guides](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/lost-and-found-co/juju-locations-collectibles) is a reasonable starting point; it names busier stages such as *Apartment Apocalypse*, *Smoothie Showdown*, and *What An Unlucky Day* as the ones where holding Juju for a late sweep pays off most.

## A Community-Confirmed Example: The Missing Meow Cat Food Bag

One location is documented well enough in the discussion thread to describe in words. A player stuck on *Missing Meow* asked about a Juju that resembles a **bag of cat food next to a television**. Another user confirmed it sits in the same room as the so-called "cat lady," and that the secret room holding it is **under a sofa where an employee is sitting** — a spot several players in the thread admitted walking straight past.

Two clarifications from that exchange are worth keeping in mind: "cat lady" refers to a woman in the level, not the elegant white cat, and the Juju is close to her room but not inside it. That kind of plain-language description is rare for this game, which is why the thread is still being consulted months after it was posted.

## How to Use Juju Hints Without Wasting Them

Collecting Juju is only half the job — spending them badly is how players end up stuck anyway. The routine that works:

1. **Play normally first.** Pick up Jujus as you sweep a room rather than stopping the level to hunt every one.
2. **Use the free hint before the paid one.** The standard clue costs nothing; a Juju hint is a limited resource.
3. **Spend only when you are down to one stubborn objective.** A hint that narrows a whole messy room is worth far more than one spent in the first two minutes.
4. **Search the area the hint points to before buying another.** Re-scan that corner in a steady pattern instead of clicking across the whole stage again.

A late sweep also makes Jujus easier to spot, simply because the obvious targets are gone and the odd grey, wobbling detail has nothing left to hide behind.

## FAQ

**Where can I find a complete list of Lost and Found Co Juju locations?**
The most complete references are the two player-made Steam guides — Lotus's chronologically ordered guide with the Autumn Update and Bliccer's demo-era list — both linked from the Steam guides hub for the game. They use screenshots rather than text descriptions.

**How many Jujus are in each level?**
The sources do not give a reliable per-level count, so treat any specific number you see elsewhere with suspicion. Players in the Steam thread have asked specifically about the final Juju in *The Missing Captain* and the last one in *Missing Meow*, which suggests the tail end of a stage is where most hunts stall.

**Why can't I see a Juju that should be there?**
Check two things: whether it is hidden behind a prop that needs clicking first, and whether the room has a secret area you have not opened yet. The *Missing Meow* cat food bag, for example, sits in a hidden room beneath a sofa.

**What do Jujus actually do?**
They are optional collectibles that convert into extra, more direct hints. Spend them on the last few objectives that refuse to turn up rather than the moment a stage starts feeling crowded.
