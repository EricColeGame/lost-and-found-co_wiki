## Is There a Rare Beetle Collectible in Lost and Found Co.?

If you are hunting for rare beetles in *Lost and Found Co.*, the honest answer is that no verified source currently documents them. We checked the official Steam store and community hub, the full Steam guides directory, a published beginner walkthrough, and a stage-by-stage walkthrough covering Chapters 8–9 — none of them mention a beetle, a beetle category, or any insect-specific collectible.

That matters because *Lost and Found Co.* is a hidden object game built on exactly this kind of confusion. Items are tucked into shelf seams, drawers, and prop clusters, and the community regularly gets stuck on a single object for days. Chasing a collectible that isn't in the game wastes the one resource you can't get back: time spent scanning rooms that never contained it.

What the evidence *does* support is a clear picture of the collectible types the game actually uses, which guides track them, and how to search efficiently. That is what this article covers.

## What Collectibles Actually Exist in Lost and Found Co.

*Lost and Found Co.* is described on its [official Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2101390/) as a hidden object adventure set in a cozy world, where you find lost items, solve puzzles, and help a tiny dragon regain her power. The collectible-style content breaks down into a few recognizable buckets.

- **Juju** — optional objectives disguised as ordinary objects. Catching them grants extra hints for later item hunts. These are the closest thing the game has to a "rare find," and multiple community guides exist solely to track them.
- **Optional objectives** — extra goals inside a level, separate from the main case. Examples documented in *My Summer Memories* include the "X marks the spot!" dig spots and a set of paint buckets.
- **Instapix** — a stage-specific collectible. In *The Accursed Antique Shop*, the Instapix is the "cursed doll," obtained by pulling a small lever near the bottle shelf, revealing a door, and touching the display case.
- **Quest items** — main and optional objective objects that unlock further targets once found. These are the bulk of what walkthroughs list.
- **Request items** — side-job objects, covered by a dedicated community guide.

Notably, the detailed stage lists we reviewed — the Missing Meow cat hunt, the antique shop inventory, the summer garden tools and seed packets, the Muddy Situation and Smoothie Showdown rounds — contain no beetles of any kind.

## How to Search for Hard-to-Find Collectibles

Whether you are chasing a Juju or a stubborn last objective, the [Casual Game Guides beginner walkthrough for Lost and Found Co.](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/lost-and-found-co) makes a useful point: the answer is rarely to click faster. Most misses come from awkward hitboxes and partially visible objects, not from bad eyes.

**Scan in layers.** Start with the skyline and hanging props, move to shelves and counters, then finish with floor clutter. On multi-floor maps, clear one zone completely before moving on.

**Use the camera tools.** Zoom in on tiny items, pan across packed areas instead of clicking blind, and switch floors the moment furniture starts overlapping.

**Open props first.** "Obscured" objectives hide inside or behind something clickable — signs, shelf lips, window frames, trays, drawers, and boxes. If a clue feels too vague, the real task is usually opening the container.

**Save hints for last.** Juju hints work best when you have already narrowed the problem to one item or one zone. Spending them early wastes the pointer.

**Trust shape over wording.** Localization can make an object name sound stranger than it is. A folded bill can register as a wallet; a clue that reads oddly often makes sense once you find the right prop cluster.

## What to Do If You Think You've Found a Beetle

If you have a screenshot of an insect-like object in a *Lost and Found Co.* level, the productive move is to report it rather than assume you're missing something. The developers' own patch notes direct players to the Steam community hub or the official Discord server when something looks wrong.

Two details from the official patch notes are worth keeping in mind:

- Patch 1.2.1 (posted October 14) lists "Added Items in Kit Shop" under general updates, alongside localization changes and several crash and UI fixes. It does not mention beetles or any new collectible category.
- The same patch fixed a slime objective in Fangtastic Feast that failed to display correctly when re-entering the stage — proof that objective display bugs do happen and are worth reporting.

Also note that not everything in the game is a collectible. Side quests feed a popularity meter that gates story progress; the Into Indie Games walkthrough notes you need enough popularity from side quests before Stage 8 unlocks. Optional objectives, by contrast, are purely for completionists.

## FAQ

**Are there rare beetles in Lost and Found Co.?**
No verified source — including the official Steam page, the community guides directory, or published walkthroughs — documents a rare beetle collectible. The game's tracked collectibles are Juju, optional objectives, Instapix, and quest items.

**What is the rarest collectible in Lost and Found Co.?**
The game does not publish rarity tiers in any source reviewed. Juju are the most guide-tracked optional find, since they are disguised as ordinary objects and grant hints for later searches.

**How many Juju are there in total?**
No official total appears in the sources reviewed. Community guides such as Lotus's Juju location guide track them level by level in chronological order, which is the practical way to confirm you haven't missed one.

**Do I need to find every optional collectible to finish the game?**
No. Main objectives drive the story forward, while optional objectives and Juju are extras. You do need enough popularity from side quests to unlock later stages, so side content isn't purely cosmetic.
