## What Is Lost and Found Co?

If you have been circling this one since the Wholesome Direct trailer and wondering whether it justifies the price, here is the short answer: **Lost and Found Co** is a polished, content-rich hidden object game that critics and players have received warmly, and its main friction point is how much story it wraps around the searching.

Developed and published by Bit Egg Inc. (with Gamirror Games), the game launched on PC and Mac on **March 5, 2026**. Steam lists it as a casual indie hidden object adventure with a cozy, hand-drawn world, and it is Steam Deck Verified. At the time of writing, the game is $17.99, discounted 20% to $14.39 in a promotion running through November 9.

Quick facts worth knowing before you buy:

- **Genre:** Hidden object adventure with point-and-click, puzzle, and decorating elements
- **Developer/Publisher:** Bit Egg Inc. / Bit Egg Inc., Gamirror Games
- **Release date:** March 5, 2026
- **Platforms:** Windows and macOS
- **Steam Deck:** Verified
- **Languages:** English interface and full audio, plus six additional subtitle/interface languages, including Korean localization added post-launch

You can check current pricing, system requirements, and the demo on the [official Lost and Found Co. Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2101390/Lost_and_Found_Co/).

## Story: Ducky, Goddess Mei, and a Magical Startup

The premise is genuinely odd in a charming way. You play Ducky, a duck who has been transformed into a human and now works as an intern at Goddess Mei's company — a magical startup devoted to reuniting townspeople with their lost belongings. Helping clients apparently helps Mei regain her power, which gives the whole item-hunting loop a narrative excuse.

Reviews consistently point out that this is unusual for the genre. Screen Hype noted that hidden object games frequently skip story entirely, while Lost and Found Co. establishes a clear throughline from the first level and keeps it going. The developer is a Thai team, and the game leans into Southeast Asian folklore — tanuki and other yokai show up across the levels.

Not everyone loves the narrative density. A long-running [Steam Community discussion titled "Way too much story"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2101390/discussions/0/563626489222808858/) captures the split: the original poster quit over the volume of exposition, while multiple commenters argued the dialogue is what makes the world worth inhabiting and suggested an optional skip for purists. iDream of Indie landed in the middle, calling the plot cute but forgettable — a pleasant excuse to string levels together rather than the main draw.

## Gameplay: Hidden Object Hunting With Juju Hints

The core loop will be familiar to anyone who has played Hidden Folks or the Hidden Cats series. Each level is a densely illustrated scene, and you scan it for the items listed along the bottom of the screen. What separates this one is how much of the scenery reacts.

**Interactive hiding spots.** Some targets are tucked inside drawers, vending machines, or other containers that only open when you click them. Items that require this are flagged with an icon, so the game is at least transparent about when something is hidden *out* of plain sight.

**Bonus objectives.** Beyond the main list, levels hand out optional tasks — help the tanuki put on a show, locate seven cryptids, warm a pot of tea for guests. These reward curiosity rather than pure scanning speed.

**Juju.** Scattered through each level are grey, wobbling objects possessed by spirits. Click them and you bottle a dark purple juju spirit, which you can later spend on a hint for an item you are stuck on. Because the supply is limited per level, hints feel earned rather than free — a design choice Screen Hype specifically praised.

**Progression gating.** To advance the story you need to earn enough "Likes" on your business's Instapix page and find every major item in previous levels. That is where the most common complaint surfaces: hunting for one stubborn item you missed can stall momentum. Both Screen Hype and iDream of Indie flagged hints that occasionally miss the mark or mislead.

**Decorating.** Grateful clients send gifts, and your earnings buy more, all of which you can place, rotate, and arrange in Ducky's room and office. It is optional in spirit but adds a second, gentler progression track.

## Art, Animation, and Sound

The presentation is the near-unanimous highlight. Every item in every scene is animated — click something, correct or not, and it wobbles, opens, squeaks, or otherwise responds. Reviewers describe stumbling into small scripted moments, like a tiger leaping onto a table or a dance troupe performing, purely by poking at the environment.

Between levels, comic-style panels carry the plot, which keeps the visual rhythm varied. The soundtrack is deliberately unobtrusive: cheerful, background-friendly music that supports the art without competing with the clicking. GamingFurever compared it to upbeat elevator music and Wii menu ditties — a compliment in this context, since a demanding score would fight the searching.

## Pros and Cons Before You Buy

**Reasons to buy:**

- Gorgeous, hand-drawn levels where nearly everything reacts to your cursor
- Substantial content — reviewers report a dozen-plus hours before deep side content
- A real narrative and characters, unusual for the genre
- Decorating system that gives your earnings somewhere to go
- Steam Deck Verified with mouse-only accessibility options

**Reasons to hesitate:**

- Dialogue-heavy pacing frustrates players who want pure searching
- Story progression requires finding every major item in prior levels
- Hints occasionally confuse rather than clarify
- If you want action or challenge over relaxation, this is not that game

## FAQ

**Is Lost and Found Co. worth it for someone new to hidden object games?**

Reviewers who described themselves as genre newcomers came away converted. The game has no penalty for clicking random objects, bonus objectives reward curiosity, and the juju hint system gives you a way out when you stall. If you like cozy, low-stress games, this is an easy recommendation — the main adjustment is the amount of reading between levels.

**How long is Lost and Found Co.?**

iDream of Indie reported at least a dozen hours of play without doing much side content, and noted a large volume of optional requests alongside the main story. Because side content is substantial and decorating is open-ended, playtime scales with how much of it you chase.

**Does Lost and Found Co. work on Steam Deck?**

Yes. Steam lists the game as Steam Deck Verified, and community posts in cozy gaming groups specifically praised how it plays on the handheld. It also supports a mouse-only control option and can be played without quick-time events.
