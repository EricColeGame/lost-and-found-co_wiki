## Love After Life at a Glance: Grimstone Grave

Love After Life is the Lost and Found Co. request stage set in **Grimstone Grave**, and it is the kind of level where a vague clue can cost you twenty minutes of staring at headstones. On paper the job is small — four objective groups — but the stage spreads those groups across a graveyard full of props, containers, and overlapping scenery.

The community-maintained objectives checklist files Love After Life under **Request stages** rather than story cases, which puts it in the game's side-job track: optional cleanup you take on for completion rather than a chapter that pushes the main adventure forward. That matters for how you play it. If you are chasing 100%, you want every group cleared; if you are only passing through, the stage is not blocking your story progress.

Two structural details are confirmed. First, the stage runs in **acts** — the October 14 patch notes reference optional dialogue for Ducky and Mei "during Act 2." Second, there is a **Chest** in the stage that now carries optional dialogue, so clicking the scenery is worth the detour.

## The Four Objective Groups You Need to Clear

The objective names below come from the [Casual Game Guides objectives checklist](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/lost-and-found-co/main-optional-objectives), which tracks task names rather than item coordinates. Treat them as your to-do list, not a map:

- **Collect the tools needed to bring life** — a setup group that likely gates later steps.
- **Retrieve body parts** — the group most players get stuck on, because the clue is deliberately broad.
- **Find wardrobe and beauty items** — a dress-up cluster, which usually means containers and clothing props.
- **Extract essential emotions** — the final group, and the one most likely to unlock after the others resolve.

Two labels from the game's objective system explain why these lists feel longer than they look. An **Obscured** objective is hidden inside or behind something clickable, so the real answer is often to open the prop first. A **Quest** objective unlocks more targets after you find it, which means finishing one step can add new entries to the same group. Do not assume a group is done the moment one item disappears.

## How to Search Grimstone Grave Without Wasting Juju

The most common failure in this stage is clicking fast instead of clicking well. Slow down, break the scene into layers, and clear one zone before moving to the next: skyline and hanging props first, then shelves, ledges, and counters, then floor clutter. In a graveyard stage, that layering habit keeps you from half-searching five areas and remembering none of them.

A few habits carry over from the rest of the game and apply directly here:

- **Zoom before you guess.** Tiny props on distant markers are easier to confirm than to click blind.
- **Open containers.** Chests, boxes, drawers, and trays are classic Obscured hosts.
- **Click the center of small items.** Awkward hitboxes cause more misses than bad eyesight does.
- **Trust shape over wording.** A clue that reads strangely may simply be a localization quirk; the art still matches.

Hold your Juju hints until you have narrowed the problem to one group or one corner of the stage. Spending a hint while six objectives are still open wastes it — the hint is most valuable when only one stubborn item remains.
